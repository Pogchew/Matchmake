# Inappropriate Behavior Reporting And Escalation Process

Status: MOSEF / high-school pilot operational draft  
Applies to: Matchmake chat, scrim activity, teams, profiles, rosters, match reviews, and other platform behavior  
Owner: `[Matchmake incident response owner]`  
Backup owner: `[backup incident response owner]`  
Reporting contact: `[support/reporting email]`  
School escalation contact: supplied by each participating school before launch

This process defines how Matchmake and participating schools should receive, triage, preserve, escalate, resolve, and document reports of inappropriate chat or team behavior. It is an operating process, not legal advice or a replacement for a school's emergency, student-safety, Title IX, civil-rights, or disciplinary procedures.

## Launch Decision

The pilot will use a school-first reporting path plus a Matchmake support escalation path. Matchmake does not currently provide an in-app report form or a dedicated report queue.

- Students should report to their coach, school esports lead, parent/guardian, trusted adult, or the school's existing reporting channel.
- Coaches and school administrators should send platform-action requests to `[support/reporting email]`.
- A student or parent/guardian may also contact `[support/reporting email]`, but Matchmake may need to coordinate with the verified school contact before disclosing student or school-controlled information.
- The support inbox is not an emergency service and must not be presented as monitored continuously.
- Reports must be tracked in a restricted case log outside the app until a dedicated queue exists. Do not place student-safety details in a public issue tracker.

Before the pilot starts, Matchmake must fill in the reporting contact, incident owners, business hours, participating-school contacts, and approved restricted case-log location.

## Immediate Safety

Do not wait for Matchmake support when anyone may be in immediate danger.

- Call 911 or local emergency services for an imminent threat, credible threat of violence, stalking with immediate danger, or another emergency.
- Follow the school's emergency and student-safety procedures and notify the designated school safety lead.
- In the United States, call or text 988 for immediate suicide or mental-health crisis support. Call 911 when there is immediate physical danger.
- Report suspected child sexual exploitation to the appropriate school safety lead and law enforcement process. The National Center for Missing & Exploited Children accepts reports through the CyberTipline.
- Do not ask a reporter to download, forward, or email suspected child sexual abuse material. Preserve only the minimum identifiers, URLs, timestamps, and account/context information needed for the authorized response, restrict access, and follow counsel-approved reporting procedures.

Matchmake support can assist with platform containment and records after the emergency path has been activated, but it must not delay emergency action.

## What Should Be Reported

Report behavior such as:

- Bullying, repeated targeting, intimidation, threats, or retaliation.
- Hate speech or harassment based on race, color, national origin, shared ancestry, sex, disability, age, religion, or another protected or school-policy category.
- Sexual content, grooming, sexual solicitation, exploitation, or sharing intimate images.
- Doxxing, stalking, impersonation, or disclosure of private student information.
- Unsafe profile links, names, logos, roster entries, scrim listings, chat messages, or match-review notes.
- Cheating coordination, match fixing, scams, spam, or repeated bad-faith scrim conduct.
- Attempts to evade a prior restriction or retaliate against someone who reported a concern.

Good-faith reports are protected from platform retaliation. A knowingly false report intended to harass another person may itself violate the Acceptable Use Policy, but an unsubstantiated report is not automatically a false report.

## Information To Include

Collect only what is reasonably necessary:

- Reporter's name and contact information, or note that the school accepted an anonymous report.
- School, organization, team, and a verified school contact.
- What happened and why it is concerning.
- Date, time, time zone, and whether the conduct is continuing.
- Relevant Matchmake URL, scrim request ID, message ID, team ID, user display name, or other record identifier when available.
- Screenshots or copied text when safe and appropriate.
- Whether anyone may be in immediate danger and which emergency or school contacts have already been notified.
- Requested platform action, if known.

Do not require a student to confront the reported person or continue an unsafe conversation to collect evidence. Do not promise anonymity; explain that information will be limited to people who need it to respond, subject to school policy and law.

## Severity And Response Targets

These are pilot operating targets, not guarantees. The incident owner records the received time, severity, reason, and next review time in the restricted case log.

### Severity 0 — Emergency

Examples: imminent danger, credible threat of violence, active self-harm risk, suspected child sexual exploitation, or active stalking/doxxing that creates immediate danger.

- Reporter/school action: use emergency, crisis, school-safety, and legally required reporting channels immediately.
- Matchmake action: when received, acknowledge as soon as seen, preserve available platform context, assign the incident owner immediately, and consider temporary containment without waiting for a complete investigation.
- Do not rely on the Matchmake support inbox as the first or only response.

### Severity 1 — Urgent Safety Or Civil-Rights Concern

Examples: non-imminent threats, sexual harassment or grooming concerns, discriminatory harassment, doxxing, stalking, repeated targeted bullying, retaliation, or evasion of a safety restriction.

- Acknowledgement target: within four business hours.
- Initial containment/coordination target: same business day.
- The school safety, Title IX, civil-rights, or other designated lead should be notified according to school policy.

### Severity 2 — Standard Conduct Violation

Examples: isolated insults, profanity directed at another user, spam, misleading listings, unsportsmanlike chat, cheating allegations without an immediate safety concern, or an inappropriate profile/link.

- Acknowledgement target: within one business day.
- Initial review target: within two business days.
- Escalate to Severity 1 if the review shows targeting, repetition, discrimination, threats, retaliation, or a student-safety risk.

If the facts are unclear, choose the higher severity until a qualified owner completes triage.

## Case Workflow

1. **Receive and stabilize.** Confirm whether anyone is in immediate danger. Direct emergencies to the appropriate emergency and school channels before routine intake.
2. **Create a case record.** Assign a case ID and record the minimum intake information, received time, source, severity, incident owner, school contact, and access restrictions in the approved restricted case log.
3. **Acknowledge.** Tell the reporter or verified school contact that the report was received, provide the case ID, state the next expected update time, and avoid promising a specific outcome.
4. **Preserve relevant records.** Record stable identifiers and preserve authorized copies or exports before destructive moderation. Limit access to the incident owner and people required for safety, legal, privacy, or school coordination.
5. **Triage and contain.** Assess severity, recurrence, affected users, and whether temporary platform restrictions or content removal are needed. `docs/admin-removal-guidance.md` governs how current controls are used.
6. **Coordinate with the school.** The school owns its student-safety, parent/guardian communication, discipline, mandated-reporting, Title IX, civil-rights, and law-enforcement decisions. Matchmake owns platform access, platform records, and enforcement of its policies.
7. **Review fairly.** Review the relevant records and context, document material facts and uncertainties, avoid unnecessary disclosure, and use a different reviewer if the assigned owner has a conflict of interest.
8. **Decide and act.** Record the platform decision, approver, actions, reason, effective time, evidence preserved, school coordination, and any follow-up monitoring. Use the least disclosure necessary.
9. **Update the reporter.** Confirm that review is complete or ongoing and describe any information that can appropriately be shared. Do not disclose another student's private information, discipline, or sensitive safety details.
10. **Close and follow up.** Record the closure reason, outcome category, school notification, reporter notification, retention/review date, and any recurrence-prevention action. Reopen the case if related conduct or retaliation occurs.

## School And Matchmake Responsibilities

### Participating School

- Publish its reporting and emergency contacts to students, parents/guardians, coaches, and administrators.
- Identify a primary and backup esports/safety contact for Matchmake.
- Handle student welfare, school investigation, discipline, parent/guardian contact, mandated reporting, and civil-rights obligations under applicable policy and law.
- Tell Matchmake when platform records or platform action are needed, using the case ID where possible.
- Prevent and respond to retaliation under school policy.

### Matchmake

- Maintain the reporting contact and restricted case log.
- Preserve and provide authorized platform records to verified school contacts, subject to privacy, legal, and contractual requirements.
- Apply platform restrictions or content actions consistently under the Terms and Acceptable Use Policy.
- Keep report access limited, document decisions, and avoid exposing student data in tickets or broad internal channels.
- Notify the verified school contact when a report may create a student-safety or civil-rights concern, unless emergency, legal, or safety considerations require another sequence.
- Monitor for recurrence or retaliation when technically practical.

## Evidence, Privacy, And Retention

- Preserve the original context before deletion or editing when it is safe and lawful to do so.
- Record stable identifiers, timestamps, and who collected each item. Do not alter screenshots or copied text used as evidence.
- Keep the case log and evidence separate from ordinary product analytics and public engineering trackers.
- Limit collection and disclosure to what is needed for safety, investigation, school coordination, legal obligations, and policy enforcement.
- Do not send sensitive student information through unapproved channels.
- Apply the retention period approved in the final school agreement, privacy policy, and incident-response policy. Until that period is approved, set a case-specific review date and do not promise permanent retention.
- A request to delete school data must be coordinated with the incident owner when records are under an active safety, legal, or investigation hold.

## Closure Outcomes

Use one or more consistent outcome labels in the case log:

- Substantiated — platform action taken.
- Substantiated — school action only or no additional platform action needed.
- Inconclusive — insufficient information after reasonable review.
- Not substantiated — available records do not support the reported violation.
- Referred — emergency, law enforcement, NCMEC, school safety, Title IX, civil-rights, privacy, or legal process owns the next action.
- Duplicate or related — linked to an existing case.
- Withdrawn — reporter withdrew, while noting that a safety or legal review may still continue.

Closing a Matchmake case does not prevent a school, regulator, law-enforcement agency, or other authorized body from continuing its own process.

## Current Product Limitations

- There is no student-facing in-app report form.
- There is no dedicated report/case database or abuse queue.
- The owner dashboard can inspect platform records and perform some moderation actions, but its activity log is not a complete report case file or evidence archive.
- Email or school intake must be manually assigned a case ID and copied into the restricted case log.
- Response targets depend on the staffed reporting contact and business hours that still need to be filled in before launch.

These limitations should be disclosed to pilot schools. A future in-app report flow should collect stable record IDs, protect reporter privacy, route by severity, support restricted evidence, and create an auditable case without exposing reports to ordinary organization users.

- Collect a primary and backup school safety/esports contact for every pilot school.

- Approve evidence and case retention periods.
- Prepare acknowledgement, school-handoff, status-update, and closure templates.
- Train the Matchmake owner and school pilot contacts on this runbook and `docs/admin-removal-guidance.md`.
